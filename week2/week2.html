Raster and Vector graphics

Digital images can broadly be divided into raster and vector formats. Raster images are made up of pixels, meaning they are resolution-dependent. When scaled up, they lose clarity and become pixelated. Common raster file formats include JPEG, PNG, GIF, and TIFF. Vector graphics, on the other hand, are created using mathematical equations rather than pixels. Because of this, scaling a vector image does not affect its resolution or quality. Vector formats are commonly used in design and fabrication workflows, especially in CAD. Common vector file formats include DXF, AI, SVG, and EPS. Raster images can be converted into vector graphics through a process known as bitmapification. This conversion works best when the original raster image has high contrast, as the software relies on clear edges to generate vector paths.
